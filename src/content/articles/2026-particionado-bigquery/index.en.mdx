Partitioning a table in BigQuery sounds like an obvious optimization, but a poor choice of column can cost more than doing nothing. The starting point is understanding how queries are billed: you pay for the data they read, so the goal is for each query to read only what it needs.

## Pick a column your queries already filter on

The best candidate is usually a date or timestamp that appears in almost every `WHERE` clause of your reports. If nobody filters on that column, partitioning adds nothing.

```sql
SELECT order_id, amount
FROM `project.sales.orders`
WHERE order_date BETWEEN '2026-09-01' AND '2026-09-30';
```

## Clustering as a complement

When queries filter on other high-cardinality columns, such as country or customer, clustering sorts the data inside each partition and reduces what gets read even further.

## Measure before and after

Before changing anything, check how much your most frequent queries read today, then compare after the change. Without that measurement, it is easy to optimize what does not hurt.
