Giving feedback is one of the skills we practice most and train least. Conversations often go wrong because the person feels judged before they understand what is being asked of them.

## Separate the fact from the interpretation

Describe what happened, without adjectives: "in the last meeting you interrupted your colleague twice". Then share the impact it had: "and the proposal never got properly discussed".

## End with a concrete action

Useful feedback closes with something the other person can do differently. Ask what they think, and agree on one concrete step, even a small one.

## To practice

In class we work on this with role-play: one person gives the feedback, another receives it, and the group checks whether the message was specific and actionable.
